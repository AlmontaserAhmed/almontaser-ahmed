How it worked

four rules, both platforms
01

One library, two platforms

Same weekly wallpaper drop, cut two ways: character art plus a phone mockup for Threads, a 9-second reveal clip for TikTok. One production pass, not two.

02

Picking the character is the job

Every post used the same format — one character, one hook, one reveal — so the only real variable was the pick. It came from the previous week's numbers: which fandoms replied and reposted most, and which art style was winning — a newspaper-collage test beat the house style both times it ran. Sanji, the Tanjiro and Giyu pair, the Neymar clip — none of it was thrown at the wall.

03

Built for each platform's real incentive

Threads rewards showing up every day; TikTok rewards one post landing, whoever posted it. Same library, aimed at two different games — and it scored in both.

04

Measured weekly, doubled down on what worked

Reach, engagement, follower growth and installs got checked every week on both platforms, and the hours followed the returns: when TikTok started paying back more per post, it got more of my time and delivered the biggest number on this page. The same weekly loop — change the hook, change the call to action, read the numbers — took beta sign-up conversion up tenfold.

What this shows

Two platforms, five accounts, one person, under a year.

01

The selection process is the transferable part

TikTok's For You feed distributes on how a post performs, not on follower count — so a 762-follower account could put up a 1.1M-view video. The weekly read-the-numbers-then-pick loop behind it is the same on any brand and any platform.

02

All of it with one person and no budget

Every number on this page came from organic content — no paid push, no agency, no team. Five accounts across two platforms, and I was the whole social team.
